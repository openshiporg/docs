Openfront Restaurant is the restaurant version of Openfront. It covers the main restaurant loop: publish a menu, take orders from the storefront or from staff tools, route them to kitchen stations, run the floor, close the check, and reconcile the day.

<Callout type="warn">
Before using real service or payment data, test the payment providers you plan to use, refunds, webhooks, role permissions, API-key scopes, and the deployed order flow against disposable data.
</Callout>

## How the order lifecycle works

<Steps>
<Step>
### A guest or staff member starts the order
Guests order through the storefront. Staff open orders from the POS or add items to a table from the service floor. Every path writes a `RestaurantOrder` with `OrderItem` rows. Item rows keep snapshots of name, price, modifiers, and kitchen station.
</Step>
<Step>
### Payment is handled per channel
In the storefront, the guest picks a payment provider first (`initiatePaymentSession`). Then `completeActiveCart` checks or captures the payment and creates the order, its items, and a `Payment` in one database transaction. Staff orders are paid at the end through the POS payment screen (cash, card, gift card, or split tenders).
</Step>
<Step>
### The order enters the kitchen
Orders in `sent_to_kitchen`, `in_progress`, or `ready` get `KitchenTicket` records, one per station. POS orders are created as `sent_to_kitchen`. Online orders paid by card start as `sent_to_kitchen`. Online orders using the manual provider start as `open`.
</Step>
<Step>
### Front of house keeps the floor moving
Hosts work the waitlist and reservations. Servers use the service floor to add items, fire or hold courses, split or merge checks, combine or transfer tables, and close checks.
</Step>
<Step>
### The day closes
When an order reaches `completed` or `cancelled`, the app syncs kitchen tickets, depletes recipe ingredients, and releases tables. A failed step is logged for recovery. Managers then reconcile cash, refunds, and processor statements on the Cash & Close page.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Tenancy

One deployment runs one restaurant. `StoreSettings` is a singleton. There is no organization, restaurant group, or location model. Orders, menu, inventory, tables, payments, and staff are not split by tenant. `InventoryLocation` is only a storage location for ingredients, not a restaurant location.

Access is controlled by the user's `Role`. Roles carry flags such as `canAccessDashboard`, `canManageOrders`, `canManagePayments`, `canManageProducts`, `canManageInventory`, `canManageKitchen`, `canManageTables`, `canManageStaff`, `canManageSettings`, `canManageOnboarding`, `canManageGiftCards`, and `canManageDiscounts`, each with a matching read flag where relevant. API keys add scopes on top of the owner's role.

## Data model

The Keystone lists in `features/keystone/models` are:

| Area | Lists |
|---|---|
| People and access | `User`, `Role`, `ApiKey`, `Address` |
| Store | `StoreSettings` |
| Floor | `Floor`, `Section`, `Table`, `Reservation`, `WaitlistEntry` |
| Menu | `MenuCategory`, `MenuItem`, `MenuItemImage`, `MenuItemModifier` |
| Orders | `RestaurantOrder`, `OrderItem`, `OrderCourse`, `OrderAdjustment`, `ManagerApproval` |
| Kitchen | `KitchenStation`, `PrepStation`, `KitchenTicket`, `KitchenTicketEvent`, `KitchenMessage` |
| Cart and checkout | `Cart`, `CartItem`, `PaymentCollection`, `PaymentSession` |
| Payments | `PaymentProvider`, `Payment`, `Refund`, `Receipt`, `PaymentWebhookEvent`, `IdempotencyKey` |
| Promotions | `Discount`, `DiscountRule`, `GiftCard`, `GiftCardTransaction` |
| Inventory | `Vendor`, `InventoryLocation`, `Ingredient`, `Recipe`, `StockMovement`, `PurchaseOrder`, `WasteLog` |
| Staff | `Shift`, `TimeEntry`, `TipPool` |
| Audit | `AuditEvent` |

Several features do not have their own list. Business days, cash movements, settlements, notifications, campaigns, privacy requests, and time-correction requests are all stored as `AuditEvent` rows.

## Current limits

- One restaurant per deployment. No multi-location support.
- Stripe, PayPal, and manual adapters exist. Behavior differs by provider. Test sessions, capture, refunds, and webhooks for the ones you enable.
- Discounts have models and an admin list, but no order path applies a discount code. Only the pickup discount from store settings is applied at checkout.
- The waitlist does not send SMS. Hosts contact guests by hand and confirm it in the app.
- Email (order confirmations, campaigns) is queued and sent only when an operator triggers sending from the dashboard. SMTP acceptance is not proof of delivery.
- The POS saves a draft on the device when offline, but it does not send orders offline. There is no printer or device management.
- There is no loyalty program, delivery dispatch integration, accounting export, or payroll integration. Time clock and tips export CSV only.

## Stack

- Next.js 16 with the App Router, React 19
- KeystoneJS 6 for lists, auth, and GraphQL
- PostgreSQL through Prisma
- Stripe, PayPal, and manual payment adapters
- Tailwind CSS and shadcn/ui
- An MCP-backed AI assistant in the dashboard

Route files under `app/` are thin. Product logic lives in `features/platform` (operator screens), `features/storefront` (customer screens), and `features/keystone` (models, access, custom GraphQL operations).
