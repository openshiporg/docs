Openfront Restaurant links menu sales to ingredient cost and stock. Every stock change is written as a `StockMovement`.

## Screens

| Route | What it does |
|---|---|
| `/dashboard/platform/ingredients` | Ingredient list by category, stock counts, transfers between locations, and operations recovery |
| `/dashboard/platform/inventory/purchase-orders` | Purchase orders, status changes, receiving, invoices, credits, and lots |
| `/dashboard/platform/inventory/recipes` | Recipes and food cost. Not in the sidebar |
| `/dashboard/platform/inventory/waste` | Waste log and reversals |

Vendors and inventory locations are managed through the generic list screens (`/dashboard/vendors`, `/dashboard/inventory-locations`).

## What is in the inventory layer

### Ingredients
- current stock and unit (g, ml, kg, lb, oz, liter, gallon, each, case, box)
- category (produce, meat, dairy, dry goods, beverages, spices, seafood, other)
- par level, reorder point, and reorder quantity
- cost per unit
- vendor and storage location
- expiration date and SKU

### Recipes
- the linked menu item
- ingredients as JSON: `{ ingredientId, quantity, unit }` per row
- yield, prep time, and instructions
- calculated total cost, cost per serving, and food cost percentage

### Purchasing and waste
- `Vendor`, `InventoryLocation`, `PurchaseOrder`, `StockMovement`, `WasteLog`

## Stock operations

All of these need `canManageInventory` and take an idempotency key, so a retry does not double-post.

- **Receive** a purchase order with `receivePurchaseOrder`: line quantities, a reference, and optional lot code and expiry date per line.
- **Match invoices and credits** with `recordRestaurantPurchaseInvoice` and `recordRestaurantPurchaseCredit`. `restaurantPurchaseEvidence` shows received, billed, and credited amounts.
- **Lots**: `restaurantIngredientLots` lists lots for an ingredient. `setRestaurantLotHold` puts a lot on hold or releases it.
- **Count** with `countIngredientStock`: counted vs expected stock with a reason.
- **Adjust** with `adjustInventory`.
- **Transfer** with `transferRestaurantStock` between two ingredient records with the same SKU in active locations.
- **Waste** with `recordWaste` (spoilage, preparation error, overproduction, plate waste, expired, damaged, other) and undo with `reverseWaste`.

Purchase order statuses: `draft`, `sent`, `confirmed`, `shipped`, `partially_received`, `received`, `cancelled`. `transitionPurchaseOrder` moves draft to sent, sent to confirmed, confirmed to shipped, and any open status to cancelled. Received states are only set by posting a receipt.

## The practical workflow

<Steps>
<Step>
### Create ingredients
Add raw materials with unit, cost, vendor, location, and starting stock.
</Step>
<Step>
### Build recipes
Create a recipe, link it to a menu item, and add ingredient rows.
</Step>
<Step>
### Review food cost
The recipe page shows total cost, cost per serving, and food cost percentage.
</Step>
<Step>
### Close real orders
When an item is ordered, its recipe is frozen as a snapshot. When the order is completed, or cancelled after the kitchen started the item, the app writes `sale` stock movements from that snapshot.
</Step>
<Step>
### Purchase and log waste
Send and receive purchase orders, match invoices, and log waste.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Recovery

If stock depletion fails after an order closes, it is logged as needing recovery. The Ingredients page shows these (`restaurantOperationsRecovery`). Staff can retry (`retryRestaurantOperations`) or record a review for items from older versions without a recipe snapshot (`reviewLegacyRestaurantRecipes`). `reconcileOrderInventory` re-runs depletion for one closed order.

## Current gaps

- Recipe ingredients are stored as JSON, not as related records.
- Depletion fails if a recipe ingredient's unit no longer matches the ingredient's stocking unit. There is no general unit conversion.
- Recipe food cost does not feed the reports.
- There is no automatic reorder based on par levels.

<Callout type="info">
Very small recipe quantities can round to zero. Use grams or milliliters as stocking units when recipes use small amounts.
</Callout>
