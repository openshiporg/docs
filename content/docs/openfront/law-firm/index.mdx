Openfront Law Firm is an open-source app for running a law practice. Staff handle intake, conflict checks, matters, dockets, deadlines, documents, messages, time, billing, retainers, and trust records in one dashboard. Visitors can read about the firm, browse practice areas and attorneys, open an account, and request a consultation. Clients can see items the firm shares with them in a portal.

To install it, see [Getting started](/docs/openfront/law-firm/getting-started).

<Callout type="warn">
The software gives you records, controls, and audit evidence. It does not guarantee compliance with legal-ethics, privilege, trust-accounting, privacy, retention, signature, or court rules. Each firm still needs its own legal, security, and provider review.
</Callout>

## Stack

Next.js 16 (App Router) with React 19, Keystone 6 with a GraphQL API, Prisma with PostgreSQL, Tailwind CSS 4, and shadcn/ui. Sign-in uses Keystone password auth with stateless session cookies.

## Tenancy and access

`Firm` is the tenant. Every firm-owned list carries a `firm` relationship. Shared hooks set the active firm on create and reject links to records from another firm. Lists with a lifecycle check each status change against a fixed transition table.

Staff access comes from an active `FirmMembership` and that firm's `Role`. A role has 39 `can*` flags, for example `canManageIntake`, `canRunConflictChecks`, `canResolveConflicts`, `canManageMatters`, `canManageEthicalWalls`, `canManageBilling`, `canManageTrust`, `canManagePortal`, `canManageRetention`, `canManageIntegrations`, `canManageAgents`, and `canViewAudit`.

Matter-scoped lists add three more checks:

- team membership on the matter, with `canEdit`, `canBill`, and `canAccessRestricted`;
- ethical walls, which can block a user from a matter;
- `authorizedUsers` and `blockedUsers` on `Matter`, which are maintained by the team and wall mutations and cannot be written directly.

A firm administrator does not silently pass an ethical wall.

Money is stored as integer minor units with a currency code. Document versions, trust entries, matter status events, signature events, audit events, and agent action events are append-only. Trust postings are corrected with reversing transactions, not edits.

Key lifecycles:

- **Intake:** new → screening → conflict_review → consultation → decision → converted, declined, or withdrawn.
- **Conflict check:** requested → running → review_required → cleared or conflict_found.
- **Matter:** proposed → pending_conflict → open, on_hold, closing, closed; or declined.
- **Consultation:** requested → scheduled → completed, cancelled, or no_show.
- **Invoice:** draft → approved → issued → partially_paid → paid; also overdue and void.
- **Refund:** requested → approved → processing → succeeded or failed.

## Workflows

1. **Onboarding.** `completeFirmOnboarding` creates the firm, first office, administrator role, four standard roles, your membership, and a default practice area. See [Getting started](/docs/openfront/law-firm/getting-started#onboard-the-firm).
2. **Publish the site.** Publish a `PublicSitePolicy` (privacy notice, first-contact notice, about text, accounts and booking switches), mark practice areas public with a slug, fee, and length, and publish attorney profiles.
3. **Online booking.** A visitor creates an account (`registerConsultationAccount`) and books a slot (`bookClientConsultation`). This creates a contact, an intake with a submission, a consultation in `requested`, and a conflict check when an opposing party is named. Slots are 9:00 to 17:00 on weekdays. Staff confirm or change it with `scheduleConsultation` and `transitionConsultation`. The customer can cancel with `cancelOwnConsultation`.
4. **Intake to matter.** `startClientIntake`, `addIntakeParticipant`, conflict search terms and matches, `recordConflictDisposition`, `finalizeConflictCheck`, then `convertIntakeToMatter`, which creates the matter, parties, and team.
5. **Matter administration.** `setMatterTeamAccess`, `addMatterParty`, `changeMatterParty`, `transitionEthicalWall`, `setEthicalWallMember`, `changeMatterConfidentiality`, `transitionMatter`.
6. **Courts and deadlines.** Courts and dockets, `recordDocketEntry`, `verifyDocketEntry`, deadlines (`reviseDraftDeadline`, `transitionDeadline`), and tasks (`transitionLegalTask`).
7. **Documents.** `ingestDocumentVersion` uploads to the S3 bucket, `setDocumentCurrentVersion`, then `prepareSignatureRequest` and `addDocumentSigner`.
8. **Messages.** Threads, `addCommunicationParticipant`, a draft message, `prepareCommunicationDeliveries` (email only), then `queueCommunicationMessage`. The outbox worker sends email through SMTP.
9. **Time to cash.** Rate cards and rules, time entries and expenses (`transitionTimeEntry`, `transitionExpense`), `assembleDraftInvoice`, `transitionInvoice`, `recordManualPayment`, `allocatePayment`, `reversePaymentAllocation`, `requestRefund`, `reviewRefund`. Retainers use `prepareRetainerAgreement` and `transitionRetainerAgreement`.
10. **Trust.** Trust accounts and client or matter ledgers, `postTrustTransaction` (balanced, idempotent, serializable), and three-way reconciliation with `prepareTrustReconciliation`, `reviseTrustReconciliation`, `transitionTrustReconciliation`.
11. **Client portal.** `recordPortalDiscoveryPolicy`, `invitePortalAccount`, `acceptPortalInvitation`, `executePortalGrant`, `sharePortalResource` (document, invoice, or thread), `revokePortalGrant`.
12. **People.** `inviteFirmMember`, `changeFirmMemberAccess`, `updateFirmRoleAuthority`.
13. **Governance.** `transitionRetentionPolicy`, `assignDocumentRetentionPolicy`, `placeRetentionHold`, `releaseRetentionHold`, `disposeRetainedDocument`.
14. **Data.** `previewContactImport` and `applyContactImport` for contacts from CSV; `preparePracticeExport` for contacts, matters, invoices, time entries, or audit events (up to 500 rows).

Status, money, access indexes, credentials, and evidence fields are not writable through generic list updates on these lists. Changes go through the named mutations, which use a serializable transaction, an advisory lock, an idempotency key where money or access moves, and an audit event.

## GraphQL boundary

`/api/graphql` (in `pages/api/graphql.ts`) accepts POST only. It requires JSON, or multipart from the same origin for uploads. Bodies are capped at 1 MB and uploads at 10 MB and 5 files. Lists return at most 100 items per query. Query depth and fragment expansion are limited. Introspection and the playground are off, and internal error details are removed. Bearer tokens (API keys, OAuth access tokens, agent credentials) are accepted and checked against their scopes and the current owner's permissions.

## Integrations

| Area | What the code does |
|---|---|
| Document storage | Uploads go to the configured S3-compatible bucket. Downloads use signed links that expire after 5 minutes. |
| Email | Password reset, portal sign-in, and portal invitation emails use SMTP. Message deliveries are sent by the outbox worker (`features/keystone/webhooks/run.ts`) through the same SMTP settings, with up to 5 attempts. Only email deliveries can be prepared; portal and SMS delivery are not configured. |
| Webhooks | Subscriptions, signed deliveries (`x-openfront-signature` header), and attempts. The worker only contacts hosts listed in `WEBHOOK_ALLOWED_HOSTS`. |
| Provider connections | `IntegrationConnection` records for email, calendar, document storage, signature, payment, court, accounting, or other. Credentials are stored encrypted with `INTEGRATION_CREDENTIAL_KEY`. A connection record does not mean an adapter exists. |
| API keys and OAuth | `issueApiKey` shows the secret once. OAuth uses `/api/oauth/authorize` and `/api/oauth/token` with authorization code plus S256 PKCE and refresh token rotation. |
| Agents | Agent principals, credentials, and grants scoped to one firm and optionally one matter. `requestAgentAction` → `reviewAgentAction` → `executeAgentAction` for a fixed set of action types (for example `task.draft`, `matter.transition`, `invoice.issue`, `trust.post`, `communication.send`). Agents are managed in the generic Models pages, not a platform page. |
| AI chat | The dashboard AI sidebar can use a shared OpenRouter key (`OPENROUTER_*`) or a key the user enters. |
| MCP | `/api/mcp-transport/http` returns 501 `LEGAL_MCP_DISABLED`. Generic MCP tools for legal data are turned off. |

## Limitations

- **No payment provider.** Payments are recorded by hand. `recordRefundProviderOutcome` and payment provider outcomes throw "unavailable until authenticated provider ingress is configured". There is no card checkout, and consultation fees are shown but not collected.
- **No signature provider.** You can prepare a signature request and add signers. Sent and signed states need provider events, which the code refuses for now.
- **No inbound provider events.** Communication delivery outcomes, signature outcomes, and webhook attempt recording through GraphQL all throw until authenticated ingress exists.
- **Email only.** Portal and SMS message delivery are not configured.
- **No court rules.** Deadlines are entered and verified by people. Nothing calculates court deadlines or files with a court.
- **Legal AI is read-only.** The AI pages show matter evidence, a document inventory, and review sets. No model is connected for drafting, review tables, or research. The provider page says providers are not connected.
- **MCP is disabled.**
- **Anonymous intake is off.** `PUBLIC_INTAKE_ENABLED` is `false` in `features/storefront/content.ts`. Visitors must sign up to request a consultation.
- **One public firm.** The public site and booking only work when exactly one firm is active.
- **Fixed booking hours.** Consultation slots are 9:00 to 17:00, Monday to Friday, in code.
- **Outbox needs a scheduler.** Email deliveries and webhooks only go out when you run the worker script.
- **Sample data is limited.** The onboarding sample option works only on an owned offline test database.
- **Not certified.** Nothing in the repository certifies trust accounting, privacy, security, or ethics compliance.
